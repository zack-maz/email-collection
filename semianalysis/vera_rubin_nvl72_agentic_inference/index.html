Section 01

What Makes a Workload Agentic?

Most inference benchmarks were designed for chatbot traffic: a single turn, a response, done. Agentic workloads are structurally different. They are defined by four properties that together produce a very different load shape on the hardware.

Multi-turn: a session spans tens or hundreds of interactions between user and assistant — not a handful. Each turn appends to the context, so the prompt grows continuously.

Long context: system prompts, tool definitions, and the accumulating conversation make context grow fast. By the midpoint of a session, the context dwarfs any single-turn workload.

High prefix reuse: because each turn is just the previous context plus new tokens, most of the input can be served from KV cache rather than recomputed. As turns accumulate, the ratio of cached-to-fresh input approaches 1.

Sub-agent bursts: a session frequently spawns short-lived sub-agents with fresh context to handle tool calls or parallel reasoning branches. These create spiky, bursty KV-cache demand on top of the steady-state session load.

Why this matters for hardware: agentic traffic rewards large fast memory (to hold KV caches for many concurrent sessions) and high interconnect bandwidth (to move KV tensors across nodes for disaggregated prefill/decode). Both are exactly what the NVL72's 72-GPU NVLink fabric is designed for.

Section 02

Performance per TCO — the 67× Number

TCO-normalised throughput is the most important angle for inference providers: it tells you how many tokens you can generate for every dollar you spend on compute. More tokens per dollar = more revenue per dollar = more margin.

SemiAnalysis measured on the DeepSeek V4 Pro (~1.6T parameter MoE) using the AgentX agentic benchmark. The headline number — 67× better throughput per TCO at 170 TPS — requires context: it compares Vera Rubin against GB300 TRTLLM near the edge of GB300's measured range, where GB300's curve is near its steepest and the denominator is smallest. Against GB300 SGLang at 170 TPS, the multiplier is ~5.6×.

The comparison that best represents realistic production operating points is at 60–100 TPS P90 interactivity — a demanding but achievable SLA. At 80 TPS, Vera Rubin delivers 10× more tokens per dollar than a single-node B300 and roughly 1.4–3× more than GB300 NVL72.

Section 03

Performance per Watt — Jensen Sandbagging Again

Power-capped datacenters are the real constraint for the largest AI deployments. When you cannot add more circuit breakers, the only way to serve more traffic is to get more tokens per watt. This is why tok/s per MW is becoming the primary procurement metric for hyperscalers.

At GTC 2026, Jensen Huang presented a chart showing Vera Rubin NVL72 achieving 3× the performance per MW of Blackwell on a ~2T parameter MoE at 200 TPS. SemiAnalysis' pre-release measurements already show up to 7× better throughput per megawatt. This is not a first: at GTC 2024 Jensen claimed GB200 NVL72 would deliver 30× Hopper's performance; SemiAnalysis measured 98×.

The pattern is deliberate under-promise so that by the time software matures and customers deploy at scale, performance has "exceeded expectations." Useful to know when interpreting NVIDIA GTC claims.

Jensen's GTC rule of thumb: multiply any GTC performance-per-watt claim by at least 2× to get a lower bound on what production software will actually deliver. SemiAnalysis has now validated this for both Blackwell and Rubin.

Section 04

Revenue & Profit per Gigawatt

Power efficiency translates directly into business economics. An inference provider with a fixed power allocation can monetize more demand with Rubin without securing additional utility power — which is the hardest thing to get in a dense AI datacenter build.

SemiAnalysis modelled annual revenue and profit per all-in utility gigawatt for a DeepSeek V4 Pro deployment, assuming 60% utilization and no model license fee (MIT license). At 75 TPS interactivity:

  • Vera Rubin NVL72: $159.5B annual revenue, $149.9B modelled profit per GW
  • GB300 NVL72 (Dynamo SGLang): $114.9B revenue, $105.3B profit per GW

The pricing optionality: Rubin's efficiency advantage gives operators a choice — extract the gain as margin, or compete on price. At 60% utilisation and 75 TPS, Rubin could charge 28% less per token than GB300 and still generate the same revenue per GW. That pricing headroom is a competitive weapon in commoditising inference markets.
"The more you buy, the more you earn" — Jensen's claim is mechanically correct for power-constrained operators. Every watt of Rubin capacity generates more billable tokens than the same watt of any prior generation. The constraint is always power, not chips.
